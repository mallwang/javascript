# Main Game Concept

The core concept of the game revolves around building and managing a successful brewery empire as the chief executive officer (CEO). Players will start with a limited amount of proprietary capital and can choose from different ways of progress.

## Requirements

- the game must be playable in a web browser on both desktop and mobile devices
- the game is a single-player experience with asynchronous multiplayer elements
- the game must have a persistent world that continues to evolve even when the player is offline
- the game must be motivating and engaging over long periods of time
- the game must have a clear progression system
- the game must have competitive and cooperative elements with other players
- the game must have a dynamic economy with supply and demand mechanics
- the game requires to perform a set of actions within a given time frame to progress
- the game should also reward players for being online, even after they have completed their actions for the current time frame
- the advantage of being online should not be so significant that it creates an unfair advantage for players who can spend more time in the game

## Key Features

- **Time Progression:** The game progresses in discrete time intervals (e.g., days, weeks). Each interval allows players to make decisions regarding production, sales, and management. When all actions for the current interval are completed, players can still benefit from being online as certain processes (like brewing) continue in the background.
- **World Map:** A dynamic world map where players can expand their brewery empire by acquiring new locations, each with its own market conditions and customer base.
- **Production Management:** Players must manage the production of different beer types, each with its own recipes, costs, and production times. Upgrading equipment and facilities will improve efficiency and output.
- **Quality System:** Different beer recipes have varying quality levels that affect customer satisfaction and sales. Players can invest in research and development to create new recipes and improve existing ones. The quality levels will reach from 0 (lowest) to 100 (highest), for every property in the game. This includes ingredients, beer quality, employee skills, market reputation, and more.
- **Markets:** Players can sell their beer in different markets, ranging from local to global. Each market has its own entry requirements, requires licenses, and has differnet types of customers. The markets are influenced by demand and competition, affecting the pricing and sales volume. Markets are shared among all players, creating a dynamic economic environment.
- **Employees:** Players can hire employees from a shared unemployment pool, train them within their company (e.g. training centers that players can build) or poach them from other players. Each employee has a unique combination of skills and attributes that affect their performance in different roles, such as brewing, sales, or logistics. Players can train employees to improve their skills, making them more effective in their roles.
- **Events:** Special events, such as beer festivals, sports events or fairs, provide opportunities for players to boost their sales and brand visibility. Participating in events requires strategic planning and resource allocation.
- **Contracts and Missions:** Players can undertake contracts and missions that provide specific goals and rewards (e.g. brew a beer with certain ingredients or with a certain quality). Completing these tasks will help players progress and unlock new features.
- **Upgrades and Research:** Players can invest in upgrades for their brewery facilities and equipment, as well as research new technologies and beer recipes to stay competitive in the market.
- **Statistics and Leaderboards:** Players can track their progress through various statistics, such as total production, sales, and market share. Leaderboards will showcase the top players in different categories, fostering competition and motivation.

## Late Game Progression

After the beer market is saturated, players can explore additional marktes like spirits, non-alcoholic beverages, or international markets.

## Production

Players must manage resources such as ingredients, equipment, and facilities to produce beer. Efficient resource management is crucial to maximize production output and minimize costs. Brewing cycles take time, and players must plan their production schedules accordingly. Upgrading brewing equipment and facilities will enhance production speed and quality.

## Beer Types

Players can produce various types of beer, each with its own recipes, production times, and market demand. Different beer types will appeal to different customer segments, and players must adapt their production strategies to meet market preferences. Players can also experiment with new recipes and create unique beer blends to differentiate themselves in the market.

## Brewing Recipes

Before starting production, players must select a brewing recipe that defines the ingredients, brewing time, and quality level of the beer. Players start with no receipes and need to either research them or acquire them from research institutions.

The recipes will have different complexity levels, affecting the required brewing time and the skill level of employees needed to produce high-quality beer.

The brewing recipes for different customers and markets will be a central gameplay element, as players must adapt their production strategies to meet the diverse preferences of their target audience.

Players should be motivated to find the best recipes for their target markets, as this will directly impact their sales and profitability. When players have the maximum of market research and customer data, they should be able to identify the optimal recipes for each market segment, but this should require significant effort and investment.

Finding the best recipes should also enable players that are only seldom online to compete with players who are more active in the game, as having the right recipes can lead to higher sales and customer satisfaction even with less frequent gameplay.

## Ingredients

Players can source ingredients from various suppliers, each offering different quality levels and prices. Higher quality ingredients result in better beer quality, which can lead to increased customer satisfaction and sales. Players can also invest in research to discover new ingredients and improve existing recipes.

## Warehouses

Players can build and manage warehouses to store raw materials and finished products. Efficient warehouse management is essential to ensure a steady supply of ingredients for production and timely delivery of beer to markets. Upgrading warehouses will increase storage capacity and improve logistics efficiency.

Each brewery has its own integrated warehouse where raw materials and finished beer are stored, which is limited and can be upgraded over time. Brewey warehouses should be only act as initial storage or for intermediate storage during production but will never be sufficient for long-term storage of large quantities of beer.

The smallest warehouse size should be bigger than the biggest brewery-integrated warehouse size, to ensure that players always have to invest in external warehouses to store their beer.

After production, beer is stored in the integrated brewery warehouse first, and need to be moved to the external player's warehouse until it is sold in the markets. Players must balance production and storage to avoid overproduction or stockouts.

After a production cycle is completed, the produced beer is transferred to the brewery's integrated warehouse. From there, players can choose to move the beer to their external warehouses for long-term storage or directly allocate it to markets for sale. Efficient management of these transfers is crucial to meet market demand and optimize storage costs.

## Logistics

Players must manage the logistics of transporting beer from their brewery to various markets. Efficient logistics management is crucial to minimize transportation costs and ensure timely delivery. Players can invest in upgrading their logistics infrastructure, such as vehicles and distribution centers, to improve efficiency.

## Distribution Centers

Players can build and manage distribution centers to facilitate the transportation of beer to different markets. Distribution centers help streamline logistics operations and reduce transportation costs. Upgrading distribution centers will enhance their capacity and efficiency.

Alternatively, players can outsource logistics to third-party providers, which can be more cost-effective but may result in less control over delivery times and quality.

## World Map

The game features a dynamic world map where players can expand their brewery empire by acquiring new locations. Each location has its own market conditions, customer base, and competition levels. Players must strategically choose locations to maximize their market reach and profitability.

The world map is divided into different regions, each with unique characteristics and challenges. Players can unlock new regions as they progress in the game, providing opportunities for expansion and growth.

The world map also features various landmarks and points of interest, such as famous beer festivals, historical breweries, and scenic locations. These landmarks can provide unique opportunities for players to boost their brand visibility and sales.

The world map will be a fictional representation of the real world, with recognizable countries and cities, but with altered names and features to avoid any legal issues.

The world map will be presented in a stylized 2D format, with a focus on clarity and ease of navigation. Players can zoom in and out to explore different regions and locations, and can click on specific locations to view detailed information about the market conditions and opportunities.

## Markets

Players can sell their beer in different markets, ranging from local to global. Each market has its own entry requirements, customer preferences, and competition levels. Players must adapt their marketing strategies and pricing to succeed in each market. Markets are shared among all players, creating a dynamic economic environment where supply and demand influence prices and sales volumes.

The market range will be a central progression element in the game. As players expand their brewery and increase production capacity, they will unlock access to larger markets with higher sales potential but also increased competition and entry requirements.

Market range will be counted in the number of potential customers that can be reached. For example, a local market may have a range of 10,000 customers, while a global market may have a range of several million customers.

At the end of every week, the player will receive a report showing the performance of each market they are active in, including sales volume, revenue, customer satisfaction, and competition levels. This report will help players make informed decisions about their market strategies for the upcoming week. The numbers are only approximate values to give players an overview of their market performance without overwhelming them with too much data. For getting more precise data, players can invest in market research and analytics tools.

### Customer Types

Each market will have different customer types with varying preferences and purchasing behaviors. Players must understand the demographics and preferences of each customer type to tailor their marketing strategies effectively.

Customers are grouped into pops (part of population) based on their preferences, income levels, and purchasing behaviors. Each pop will have a certain percentage of the total market population, and players must target the right pops to maximize their sales.

Each pop will have specific preferences for beer types, quality levels, and price ranges. Players must analyze market data and customer feedback to identify the most lucrative pops and adjust their production and marketing strategies accordingly.

### Market Types

- **Local Markets:** Accessible to players with small breweries, these markets have lower entry requirements and cater to local customers. Competition is moderate, and prices are relatively stable.
- **Regional Markets:** Players with medium-sized breweries can access these markets, which have higher entry requirements and a more diverse customer base. Competition is more intense, and prices fluctuate based on supply and demand.
- **National Markets:** Reserved for players with large breweries, these markets have stringent entry requirements and attract customers from across the country. Competition is fierce, and prices are highly dynamic, influenced by national trends and events.
- **Global Markets:** Reserved for players with large breweries, these markets have the highest entry requirements and attract international customers. Competition is fierce, and prices are highly dynamic, influenced by global trends and events.

## Employees

### Unemployment Pool

Players can hire employees from a shared pool of unemployed individuals, which is accessible to all players. The pool will vary over time, with new employees becoming available and others leaving the market. This should be realistically simulate the job market, which means that the employee skills will rather be average to low (total skill level of maximum 50) as the highest skilled employees are already employed.

Sometimes, highly skilled employees (skill level between 50 and 75) may become available in the unemployment pool due to various reasons, such as layoffs or career changes. However, these instances should be rare to maintain the competitive aspect of hiring.

Whenever a player fires an employee, that employee will return to the shared unemployment pool immediately, making them available for other players to hire. This creates a dynamic job market where players can capitalize on opportunities to acquire skilled employees who have been let go by others. Once fired, the employee's skills and attributes remain unchanged, but the expected salary may decrease due to the stigma of being recently unemployed (e.g., by 20-30%).

Whenever a player hires an employee from the unemployment pool, that employee is removed from the pool and is no longer available for other players to hire. Newly hired employees will have a poaching lock-in period during which they cannot be poached by other players (e.g., 30 in-game days).

### Poaching

Players can poach employees from other players by offering better salaries or incentives. When poaching an employee, there is a chance that the current employer may try to retain them by matching the offer or providing additional benefits. The success of a poaching attempt will depend on factors such as the employee's satisfaction with their current job, the attractiveness of the new offer, and the relationship between the two players.

When an employee poaching attempt is unsuccessful and the current employee decides to stay with their current employer, the player who attempted the poach may face a temporary penalty, such as a decrease in reputation or increased costs for future poaching attempts and the employee will have a lock-in period during which they cannot be poached again for a certain duration (e.g., 30 in-game days).

Poaching should be a strategic decision, as it can lead to increased costs and potential conflicts with other players. Players must weigh the benefits of acquiring a skilled employee against the risks and costs associated with poaching. The amount of paching attempts should be limited to a certain number per time frame (e.g., 3 attempts per week) to prevent excessive poaching and maintain game balance.

### Apprenticeship Program

Players can establish apprenticeship programs within their breweries to train and develop new talent. Apprenticeships will allow players to nurture employees with lower skill levels, gradually improving their abilities over time. This program will require an initial investment and ongoing costs but will provide a steady stream of skilled employees in the long run.

This approch should be the primary method for players to develop their workforce, as it encourages long-term planning and investment in human resources.

### Employee Training

Players can invest in training programs to improve the skills of their employees. Training can be conducted in-house or through external training centers that players can build. Training programs will have associated costs and time requirements, but they will enhance employee performance and contribute to the overall success of the brewery.

The employee training requires to have certain buildings and training personnel available in the brewery to conduct effective training sessions. The installed equipment, training materials, and the expertise of the trainers will influence the effectiveness of the training programs and the gain in employee skills.

### Employee Types

- **Brewmasters:** Skilled in brewing processes, improving beer quality and production efficiency.
- **Sales Representatives:** Experts in marketing and sales, increasing market reach and customer base.
- **Logistics Managers:** Efficient in managing supply chains, reducing costs and delivery times.
- **Event Coordinators:** Specialists in organizing events, boosting brand visibility and sales during festivals.

### Employee Skills

- **Brewing Skill:** Affects the quality and efficiency of beer production.
- **Sales Skill:** Influences the effectiveness of marketing and sales strategies.
- **Logistics Skill:** Impacts the efficiency of supply chain and distribution management.
- **Event Management Skill:** Determines the success of organizing and participating in events.
- **Training Skill:** Affects the speed and effectiveness of employee training programs.

The required salary depends of the total skill level of an employee. The total salary increases exponentially with higher skill levels to reflect the increased value and expertise of highly skilled employees. Players must carefully consider their budget when hiring and managing employees and balance the costs with the benefits of having skilled personnel.

The skills will be displayed as a spider chart to give players a quick overview of the employee's strengths and weaknesses.

### Employee Attributes

- **Age:** Influences experience and potential for skill development.
- **Experience:** Affects overall performance and adaptability to different roles.
- **Loyalty:** Determines the likelihood of staying with the company and resisting poaching attempts.
- **Salary Expectation:** Influences hiring costs and retention strategies.
- **Motivation:** Affects productivity and willingness to take on additional responsibilities.

The attributes will be displayed as a list with numerical values to provide players with detailed information about each employee.

### Employee Satisfaction

Employee satisfaction plays a crucial role in retention and performance. Players must monitor and manage factors that influence satisfaction, such as workload, compensation, and work environment. Satisfied employees are less likely to be poached and will perform better in their roles.

The satisfaction level will be a combined single value ranging from 0 (very dissatisfied) to 100 (very satisfied), which is influenced by various factors such as salary, work conditions, job security, and career development opportunities.
